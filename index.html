How it works

The model A(T) = Ab·Q10(T−Tb)/10 is fitted by non-linear least squares on the activity itself (Levenberg–Marquardt), starting from a straight-line regression of ln(activity) on temperature. Q10 > 1 means activity rises with temperature.

The ± values are the square roots of the diagonal of s²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ with s² = SSE/(n−2), the same definition as SciPy’s curve_fit. The base temperature only changes Ab and its error; Q10, R² and the fitted curve stay the same.

The Arrhenius regression fits ln(activity) against 1000/T (T in kelvin) with a straight line and reports Ea = −slope × 1000 × R.

Axis scales: tick Temperature and/or Activity below the plot and enter Min and Max; press Enter, click the arrows or move to another field to apply. Temperature 0–100 °C (step 0.5 °C); activity 0 to 100 × the largest activity in the data, with the step set to the data maximum / 50 rounded to 1, 2 or 5 × 10ⁿ.

All calculations run in your browser. The data you enter is not sent or stored.
